The morning

Where the mountain meets the sea, the road becomes yours.

There is a stretch of coast where Table Mountain falls straight into the Atlantic, and for a few unforgettable hours it belongs to you alone. You take the wheel of a Ferrari, an Audi R8 or a Porsche 911 and follow the cliffs the way these roads were meant to be driven — Chapman's Peak, Clarence Drive, the sea flashing silver on every bend.

A professional lead driver runs ahead, in radio contact throughout, so there is nothing to navigate and nothing to prove. Two of you to a car, trading the wheel along the way. Just the engine, the ocean air, and the person beside you. This is not a transfer between two places — it is the part of the trip everyone remembers long after the rest has faded.

Cape Town to Arabella · one way

Three ways to drive the coast

The same glorious coastline, at three lengths — ending at Arabella, Kleinmond, with Hermanus's Old Harbour a short extension further.

Four hours
~175 km · three stops

Chapman's Peak & Clarence Drive

Out along the Atlantic seaboard through Camps Bay and Llandudno to Hout Bay, then Chapman's Peak Drive, cut into the cliff face high above the water. Coffee at Noordhoek, where the keys change hands. Over Ou Kaapse Weg to the False Bay coast and east to Gordon's Bay, then Clarence Drive — the road that runs between the mountain and the sea — with a long photography stop above Kogel Bay. Through Betty's Bay to Kleinmond, and in to Arabella.

Two of the best coastal roads anywhere, in one morning — the one we'd put in front of you.
Three hours
~145 km · two stops

Clarence Drive, the long way

Down to Muizenberg and east along the False Bay shoreline to Gordon's Bay for coffee at the harbour. Then Clarence Drive in full — Kogel Bay, Rooi Els, Pringle Bay, Betty's Bay — with a long stop at the Kogel Bay viewpoints, which is where the photographs come from. A second coffee at Kleinmond, before Arabella.

The best single piece of road on the list, without the early start.
Two hours
~130 km · one stop

Clarence Drive direct

Straight out over Sir Lowry's Pass to Gordon's Bay, then Clarence Drive to Kleinmond and Arabella, with one stop above Kogel Bay for photographs. The shortest version that still feels like a drive rather than a transfer.

Right for clients arriving that morning, or teeing off in the afternoon.

If the weather turns, an inland alternative runs the same distance through the Elgin Valley and Houw Hoek — mountains and farmland instead of ocean, and a road few visitors ever see.
